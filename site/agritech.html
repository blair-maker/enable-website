The sector is not hard because it is rural.

It is hard because the relationships are genuinely more complicated than the data models most software ships with, and because the consequences of getting it wrong land on people you will see at the saleyards.

One party, several hats

Supplier, shareholder, customer and debtor are frequently the same family entity across several trusts. Standard account hierarchies bend badly here, and the workaround usually becomes permanent.

A year that is not four equal quarters

Calving, harvest, shearing and payout cycles drive when money and attention exist. Forecasting and service coverage built on even quarters will be wrong twice a year, predictably.

Compliance that keeps arriving

Farm environment plans, freshwater rules, emissions reporting and traceability keep adding obligations. The organisations coping well treat evidence capture as part of the normal workflow rather than an annual scramble.

Two different clients, two different jobs.

Agritech companies building a product

If you sell software or services to farmers, your Salesforce problem is usually onboarding, support at scale and understanding which customers are actually getting value. We work on the commercial platform behind the product, and we know better than to redesign your product.

Primary sector organisations

Co-ops, processors, rural service businesses and industry bodies. Here the work is usually making one grower legible across supply, shares, trade and compliance, which is a data modelling job long before it is a CRM job.

How an engagement starts.

1

Map one grower end to end

Every relationship they have with you, every system that holds part of it. This is usually the first time anyone has drawn it on a single page.

2

Decide what an entity is

Farm, family, trust, supply number, shareholding. Getting this wrong is the single most expensive mistake in the sector and it is very hard to unwind later.

3

Build for the season, not the quarter

Forecasts, service coverage and campaign timing follow the production calendar, because that is when your customers have time and money.

4

Then keep improving it

Rules change, and they change on the government cycle rather than yours. Waypoint is how most clients absorb that without a new project each time.
