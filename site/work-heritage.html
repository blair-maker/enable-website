The work / Heritage and culture

One profile for a person who turned up in six systems.

A national heritage organisation could see every interaction a supporter had with it, provided you already knew which system to look in. We unified that activity onto a single profile in Data 360 and put it in front of staff.

Six systems, one person

Resolution is where this kind of project succeeds or quietly fails. It is not a field-mapping exercise.

The situation

Memberships in one place, donations in another, event attendance in a third, visits and enquiries elsewhere again. Nobody could answer a question as simple as "what has this person actually done with us" without opening several systems and making a judgement call about whether three similar records were the same human being. The organisation knew this. What it did not have was a way through it that did not begin with a multi-year data warehouse project.

What we built

01

Modelled to the Customer 360 data model

Source systems mapped onto standard objects rather than a bespoke schema, so that what we built would still make sense to the next person and would not need re-mapping every time a source changed.

02

Identity resolution that survives real data

A ruleset tuned against the actual records, including the households, the shared email addresses and the people who have been in the database since the nineties under three spellings. Match rates were reviewed with staff who knew the constituents rather than signed off on a metric.

03

Unified activity on the profile

The engagement history brought together so that a single view shows what a person has done, in order, regardless of which system recorded it. This is the part users actually notice.

04

Deployed to production, with tests

Apex covered per class, deployed with specified tests against a production org rather than left as a proof of concept somebody would productionise later.

What changed

Staff stopped guessing

The question "have we talked to this person before" is now answered on one screen instead of by three people checking three systems and comparing notes.

A foundation, not an endpoint

The unified profile is what segmentation, agent grounding and personalisation all need underneath them. Building it first made the next conversations shorter.

Data quality became visible

Resolution surfaces duplicates that everyone suspected and nobody could count. That is uncomfortable and useful in roughly equal measure.

What we would tell you

Identity resolution is where this kind of project succeeds or quietly fails, and it cannot be done well from a spreadsheet of field mappings. Budget for sitting with the people who know the constituents. Also: Data 360 consumption is real money, and we would rather model it with you before you commit than explain the bill afterwards.
