What it is, as a product

Marketing Cloud Next is not the old Marketing Cloud with a new badge. It runs on the core platform, which changes what a campaign can see and what it can trigger.

Journeys built in Flow

The same Flow your admins already know, not a separate journey canvas with its own logic and its own outages. A journey can call anything the platform can do.

Content and channels

Branded email and content on the platform, plus SMS, WhatsApp and push. Sender setup, templates and reusable content blocks that marketers maintain themselves.

Segments from Data 360

Audiences built on the unified profile rather than on a list someone exported. Membership updates as the data does, so a segment is a rule, not a snapshot.

Agentforce inside the campaign

Agents that answer the reply, qualify the response and pick up the conversation the campaign started, grounded in the same records.

The three things that decide whether it works

Data 360, the audience layer

Identity resolution, unified profiles and calculated insights. A segment is only ever as good as the resolution underneath it, and most campaigns that misfire, misfire here rather than in the creative. We model the credits before you commit, because consumption is where these programmes get expensive.

Personalisation, the moment

Real time means acting on behaviour as it happens: a browse, an abandoned form, a change in the profile. Not a list assembled three weeks after the moment has passed. This is the difference between personalisation and a mail merge, and it is the reason the platform sits where it does.

Intelligence, the measurement

Engagement scoring, Einstein assists and attribution that runs back to pipeline or to giving in the same org. Because it is one platform, the report does not begin with an export, which is usually where marketing measurement quietly stops being trusted.

Migrating from Marketing Cloud Engagement or Pardot

Most estates we look at carry a decade of journeys, half of which nobody has switched off. The migration is an opportunity to stop running those, and the plan should say so out loud.

01

Decide what actually moves

An inventory of every journey, automation and send, with an owner and a last-run date against each. A surprising amount of it gets retired rather than rebuilt, and that is the cheapest win available.

02

Data and consent before anything else

Subscriber data, preference centres, consent records and suppression lists reconciled first. Getting consent wrong in a migration is the one mistake with a regulator attached to it.

03

Deliverability and authentication

SPF, DKIM, DMARC, sending domains and IP warm-up planned before the first production send. Sending reputation takes months to build and one bad weekend to lose.

04

Parallel run, then switch

Both platforms live, results compared on real sends, then cut over. We do not do big-bang marketing migrations, and we will not lift a broken journey across so it can keep being broken.

What are you sending today, and what is it doing?

Bring the journey inventory if you have one. If you do not, that is usually the first thing worth building, and it is often where the business case comes from.
