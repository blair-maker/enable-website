The hard part is never the pipes.

Connecting a source system is a morning. Deciding that a date is a date and a person is a person, regardless of which system spelled it which way, is the project.

Harmonisation is a series of decisions

Every mapping is someone choosing which system wins. Done by an integrator alone, those choices are invisible and surface a year later as a number nobody believes. We make them in the open, with the people who own the data.

Identity resolution has no right answer

Match too loosely and you merge two real people. Match too tightly and your best supporter looks like four strangers. The ruleset is a business decision about which error you would rather explain.

Grounding is only as good as the above

Every agent that answers from your data inherits whatever you decided here. This is why we will not start an Agentforce build on a data layer nobody has audited.

Let us talk about credits, because nobody else will.

Data 360 is consumption priced, and the bill is driven by choices made during the build: how often you ingest, how much you harmonise, how wide your identity ruleset reaches and how often insights recalculate.

We model the cost before the build

A consumption estimate is part of the design, not a surprise after go-live. If the number is uncomfortable, it is far cheaper to find that out on a whiteboard.

Zero copy where it earns its place

If the data already lives in Snowflake, BigQuery or Databricks and nobody needs it resident, federating beats copying. Sometimes. It is a real trade-off, not a slogan.

Ingest what you will use

The most common waste we see is streaming everything because it was easy, then paying to store and process fields no segment has ever referenced.

How an engagement starts.

1

Profile the sources honestly

Before any modelling, we look at what is actually in the fields. This is the step that gets skipped, and it is the step that predicts the whole project.

2

Agree the survivorship rules

Which system wins for a name, an address, a consent flag. Written down, signed off, and revisited when it turns out to be wrong.

3

Resolve, then measure the errors

We look at what merged that should not have, and what stayed apart that should not have. Both lists matter, and only one of them is comfortable reading.

4

Only then, activate

Segmentation, insights and agent grounding are the easy end. They are also where a bad foundation becomes visible to customers.
