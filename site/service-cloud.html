Service problems are rarely about effort.

Support teams are usually working harder than the org deserves. The cost is almost always in the handoffs, the re-explaining, and the things nobody wrote down.

Routing that ignores skill

Round-robin is fair to the roster and unfair to the customer. Omni-Channel can route on skill, language, product and capacity, and most orgs are paying for that and using none of it.

Knowledge nobody trusts

An article written once in 2022 and never reviewed is worse than no article, because people find it and believe it. Knowledge needs an owner and a review date or it quietly becomes misinformation, including for your agents.

SLAs that exist only in the contract

If the entitlement is not modelled in the platform, nobody knows a clock is running until it has already stopped. Milestones are unglamorous and they change behaviour more than any dashboard.

Before you put an agent in front of customers.

Agentforce Service is genuinely good, and it is not a way around an unclear process. An agent trained on knowledge nobody trusts will answer confidently and wrongly, at a speed no human could manage.

Decide what it may not do

The useful question is not what the agent can answer, it is what it must hand over. Write the escalation rules before the happy path, because that is where reputational damage actually happens.

Ground it in something real

Grounding is a data problem, not a prompt problem. If your knowledge and case history are inconsistent, the agent inherits the inconsistency and states it with confidence.

Test it like a release

Agents regress when the underlying data changes, not when the code does. That needs a held-out set of real questions and a rerun every time you change grounding.
